What it is physically

Not a separate component — the reader's baseband processor controls the PA's output power level. A DAC or a switch on the PA bias reduces the carrier amplitude during command transmission. The modulation happens inside the PA stage or via a dedicated mixer before it.

What it does

The reader encodes commands using PIE (Pulse Interval Encoding). The carrier is normally at full power. To send a bit, the reader drops the power briefly. A short dip = data-0 (~1×Tari ≈ 25 µs). A long dip = data-1 (~1.5–2×Tari ≈ 37–50 µs). The tag sees these power dips as VDD sags on its charge pump output.

Why it matters

  • This is how ALL commands reach the tag — Query, ACK, Read, Write.
  • Deep modulation (100% ASK, full power drop) maximizes the tag's ability to detect bits but disrupts the tag's power supply.
  • PR-ASK (phase-reversal ASK) drops to ~50% — the tag stays powered during the dips.
  • The tag has no other way to receive data. It literally watches its power supply fluctuate.

Signal at this point

Input: Pure 915 MHz CW + digital command bitstream.

Output: ASK-modulated 915 MHz carrier — the envelope carries the data bits in PIE encoding. Still +30 dBm average.
