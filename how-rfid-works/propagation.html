What happens physically

The electromagnetic wave expands as a sphere. The same total power is spread over an area that grows as 4πd². The tag's antenna captures only a tiny fraction of this sphere. At 915 MHz, the free-space path loss at 1m is 31.7 dB — the tag receives about 1/1500th of the transmitted power.

The Friis equation

P_rx = P_tx × G_tx × G_rx × (λ/4πd)²

At 915 MHz, λ = 32.7 cm. With EIRP = +36 dBm and tag antenna gain ≈ −10 dBi (on skin):

At 0.5m: −2.6 dBm | At 1.0m: −8.7 dBm | At 1.5m: −12.2 dBm | At 2.0m: −14.7 dBm

The Monza R6 wakes up at −20 dBm, so range ≈ 3–4m in free space, ~1.3m on skin.

Why it matters

  • Path loss is the fundamental limit on read range.
  • Backscatter is WORSE: the signal must travel the path TWICE (reader→tag→reader), so round-trip loss ∝ 1/d⁴.
  • On skin, the body absorbs ~10 dB, cutting effective range roughly in half.
  • Multipath (reflections from walls, floor) adds constructive and destructive fading — ±10 dB is common.

Signal at this point

At reader: +36 dBm EIRP (4 Watts radiated).

At tag (1m): ≈ −8.7 dBm (0.13 mW). The 915 MHz carrier arrives with the same frequency, just much weaker. The tag's antenna couples ~±200 mV from this.
