What it is physically

A small block of EEPROM (Electrically Erasable Programmable Read-Only Memory) — floating-gate transistors that retain their charge without power. The Monza R6 has: 96-bit EPC, 48-bit TID, 32-bit user memory, and 64-bit reserved (passwords). Total: ~240 bits, maybe 0.01 mm² of die area.

What it does

Stores the tag's identity and responds to read requests from the state machine. The EPC bank is the main identifier — field-writeable by the reader (via the Gen2 Write command). The TID bank is factory-fused at the Impinj fab — a unique serial number burned into the silicon, impossible to change.

Why it matters

  • The EPC is what the reader receives during inventory — it IS the tag's identity to the outside world
  • Reading is fast (a few clock cycles) and costs almost no energy
  • Writing requires higher voltage (~15V internally, from an on-chip charge pump boost) and takes ~5 ms per word
  • Endurance: ~100,000 write cycles — plenty for supply chain (written once)

Signal at this point

Input: Read address from state machine (which bank, which word).

Output: The stored bits — most often the 96-bit EPC: E200 3412 7856 0000 0000 0001.
