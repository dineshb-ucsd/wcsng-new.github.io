What it is physically

An odd number of inverter gates (typically 7–11) connected in a ring: the output of the last feeds the input of the first. Each inverter is just a CMOS pair (one PMOS, one NMOS) — maybe 20 transistors total for the whole oscillator.

What it does

The ring of inverters creates a signal that propagates around the loop and inverts each time. After going all the way around (odd number = net inversion), it contradicts itself and toggles. Period = 2 × N × t_delay. With N=7 and t_delay ≈ 50 ns, frequency ≈ 1.4 MHz. This is the tag's system clock.

Why it matters

  • A crystal oscillator would cost more than the entire tag IC — this costs nothing
  • The frequency drifts ±30% with voltage, temperature, and process corners
  • This is why Gen2 specifies loose timing tolerances (BLF 40–640 kHz) — the tag can't keep precise time
  • The clock drives the state machine, the FM0 encoder, and all digital timing

Signal at this point

Input: VDD (determines speed) — no external signal.

Output: ~1–2 MHz square-ish clock. Edges are soft (RC-limited). Duty cycle roughly 50%.
