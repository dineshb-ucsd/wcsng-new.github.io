What it is physically

A ladder of 4–6 stages, each made of one diode-connected MOSFET and one MIM capacitor (~10–20 pF) on the chip die. Total area: a significant fraction of the ~0.5 mm² die. The MOSFETs have low threshold voltage (~200 mV) to rectify the small incoming RF swing.

What it does

Each stage acts as a half-wave rectifier and voltage doubler. On one half-cycle the diode conducts and charges the cap; on the next half-cycle the cap boosts the voltage for the next stage. Each stage adds roughly V_peak − V_diode_drop. After 4–6 stages, ±200 mV RF becomes ~1.2 V DC.

Why it matters

  • This is the tag's ONLY power source — there is no battery
  • The minimum input power for VDD to reach ~1.0 V sets the tag's sensitivity (−20 dBm for Monza R6)
  • Efficiency is ~20–30% — most RF energy is lost in the diode drops
  • The charge pump's input impedance IS the chip's RF impedance that the antenna must match

Signal at this point

Input: RF voltage swing of ±200 mV at 915 MHz across the chip pads.

Output: ~1.2 V DC (VDD) with small ripple at 2×915 MHz. This powers the entire digital logic, memory, oscillator, and everything else on the chip.
