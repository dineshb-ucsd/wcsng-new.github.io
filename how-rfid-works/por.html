What it is physically

A simple voltage comparator (or Schmitt trigger) that watches VDD. One input is tied to VDD through a resistive divider; the other to a fixed reference (~1.0 V). The output is a single digital signal: RESET_N (active low).

What it does

When the reader first powers the tag, VDD ramps up from 0 V over ~10–100 µs as the charge pump fills its caps. During this ramp, the digital logic must NOT run — its flip-flops would latch random garbage. POR holds RESET_N = 0 (logic held in reset) until VDD crosses ~1.0 V, then releases it. Hysteresis (~50 mV) prevents chattering if VDD wobbles near the threshold.

Why it matters

  • Without POR, the state machine could start in an arbitrary state and send garbage backscatter
  • On release, the state machine initializes to the "Ready" state per the Gen2 spec
  • If the tag moves out of range and VDD drops, POR resets everything cleanly

Signal at this point

Input: VDD voltage (0 → ~1.2 V ramp).

Output: RESET_N: LOW while VDD < 1.0 V (logic frozen), HIGH once VDD stable (logic runs).
