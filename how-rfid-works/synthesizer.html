What it is physically

An IC on the reader PCB (e.g. ADF4350 or similar). Contains a phase-frequency detector, a charge pump, and a voltage-controlled oscillator (VCO). An external loop filter (a few R-C components) sits between the charge pump and VCO.

What it does

The VCO produces ~915 MHz. A frequency divider (÷N, where N ≈ 45) divides this down to ~20 MHz. The phase detector compares this divided signal to the crystal reference. Any phase error pumps charge into the loop filter, which tunes the VCO until the phases match. Result: f_out = N × f_ref = 45 × 20.333 MHz = 915 MHz, locked to crystal accuracy.

Why it matters

  • RFID readers frequency-hop across 50 channels (902–928 MHz). The PLL retunes in microseconds to each channel.
  • The PLL's phase noise becomes the reader's phase noise floor — phase-based RFID sensing needs this to be low.
  • Without a PLL, you'd need a 915 MHz crystal (impractical — crystals don't go that high).

Signal at this point

Input: 20 MHz reference from crystal, ~300 mV.

Output: 915 MHz sine wave, ~0 dBm (1 mW). Frequency accuracy inherited from crystal (±20 ppm → ±18 kHz at 915 MHz).
