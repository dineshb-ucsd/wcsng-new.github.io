What it is physically

A GaAs or GaN FET amplifier IC on the reader board, plus an output matching network (a few inductors and capacitors). Often a multi-stage design: a pre-amp drives the final power stage. Runs warm — may have a small heatsink.

What it does

The PLL output is ~0 dBm (1 mW). The PA boosts it to +30 dBm (1 Watt) — a gain of 30 dB (×1000 in power). The RF signal controls when the FET conducts; the actual energy comes from the DC supply. The matching network transforms the FET's output impedance to 50 Ω.

Why it matters

  • The tag needs at least −20 dBm to wake up. At 1m with path loss, the reader must start at +30 dBm or higher.
  • PA efficiency determines heat dissipation and battery life (for handheld readers).
  • PA linearity matters for ASK modulation — the amplitude dips must be clean, not distorted.
  • FCC limits: max +30 dBm conducted, or +36 dBm EIRP with antenna gain.

Signal at this point

Input: 915 MHz sine, ~0 dBm (1 mW, ~630 mV peak into 50 Ω).

Output: 915 MHz sine, +30 dBm (1 W, ~10 V peak into 50 Ω). Same frequency, 1000× more power.
