What it is physically

A small quartz crystal (a few mm) cut to vibrate at a precise frequency — typically 10–20 MHz for RFID readers. The crystal is in a hermetically sealed metal can on the reader's PCB.

What it does

The crystal's piezoelectric resonance produces an extremely stable sine wave. Stability: ±20 ppm — that's ±20 Hz per MHz, so at 20 MHz it drifts less than ±400 Hz. This reference clock is what the PLL synthesizer (next step) multiplies up to 915 MHz.

Why it matters

  • Without a stable reference, the carrier frequency would drift and land on someone else's channel
  • Phase-based sensing and localization need the reader's frequency to be rock-solid — any drift shows up as a phase change that looks like real data
  • The FCC requires ±10 ppm accuracy for UHF RFID

Signal at this point

Input: Nothing — this is the source.

Output: Clean sine wave at ~10–20 MHz, a few hundred mV amplitude. Very low power (µW). This feeds the PLL.
