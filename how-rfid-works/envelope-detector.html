What it is physically

A peak detector (diode + RC filter) followed by a comparator. The diode charges a small cap to the peak of VDD; the comparator compares VDD to this slowly-decaying reference. When the reader drops power and VDD dips, the comparator output goes low — that's a bit.

What it does

The reader sends commands by ASK modulation — briefly dropping its carrier power. From the tag's perspective, VDD (the charge pump output) sags when the carrier dips. The envelope detector converts these sags into a clean digital bitstream at 40–160 kbps.

Why it matters

  • This is the tag's ONLY way to receive commands from the reader
  • The time constants must be tuned: fast enough to track the bit rate, slow enough to reject the 915 MHz RF ripple
  • The Gen2 protocol uses PIE (Pulse Interval Encoding): short power drop = 0, long drop = 1

Signal at this point

Input: VDD with tiny dips (~50–200 mV) when reader drops power.

Output: Clean digital bitstream — the reader's command (Query, ACK, Read, etc.).
