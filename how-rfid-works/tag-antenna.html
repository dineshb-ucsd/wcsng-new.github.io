What it is physically

A meandered dipole etched on a thin flex substrate (PET or polyimide). The arms are folded into a zigzag so a ~16 cm dipole fits in a ~4–8 cm label. An inductive matching loop near the feed point resonates out the chip's input capacitance (1.25 pF for Monza R6).

What it does

The antenna is the transducer between free-space EM waves and the voltage/current that feeds the chip. It's designed for conjugate matching — the antenna's impedance is the complex conjugate of the chip's (1.56 kΩ ∥ 1.25 pF → antenna must look like ~1.56 kΩ in series with enough inductance to cancel 1.25 pF at 915 MHz). Maximum power transfer happens only at this match.

Why it matters

  • A mismatch wastes received power. A 2:1 VSWR reflects 11% of the power — at threshold, that's the difference between working and not.
  • On human skin, the body detunes the antenna (shifts its resonance down and absorbs power). Tags for wearables must be designed/simulated on a body phantom.
  • Polarization mismatch between reader (circular) and tag (linear) costs 3 dB.

Signal at this point

Input: EM wave arriving at ≈ −10 dBm in free space. Tag antenna gain ≈ −10 dBi on skin.

Output: RF voltage swing of ±200 mV across the two chip pads (RF+ and RF−). This is what the charge pump will rectify.
