What it is physically

A large NMOS transistor whose gate is connected to a bandgap or diode-stack voltage reference (~1.8 V). When VDD exceeds this threshold, the MOSFET turns on and shunts excess current from VDD to ground, dissipating the energy as heat.

What it does

Clamps VDD at ~1.8 V regardless of how much RF power the antenna delivers. At 10 cm from a 1 W reader the tag might receive +10 dBm — without the shunt, VDD would spike to 3–4 V and destroy the thin oxide gates. The shunt absorbs all that excess energy.

Why it matters

  • Without it, bringing the reader close to the tag fries the chip
  • The shunt changes the chip's input impedance at high power — at close range the antenna is loaded more heavily, which affects the reflection coefficient (and thus the backscatter phase the reader measures)
  • This is one reason RFID sensors often compare the phase of two tag ICs sharing one antenna: both chips' shunts activate similarly, so the effect cancels in the difference

Signal at this point

Input: VDD from charge pump (could be 0.5 V to 4+ V depending on distance).

Output: VDD clamped at ≤ 1.8 V. Excess current shunted to GND.
