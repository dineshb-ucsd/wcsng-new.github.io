What it is physically

A small ferrite disc (the size of a coin) with three coaxial ports arranged in a Y. A permanent magnet biases the ferrite. It's a passive component — no power needed. On the reader board, it sits between the PA output and the antenna connector.

What it does

Signals can only travel clockwise through the three ports: Port 1 (TX) → Port 2 (Antenna) → Port 3 (RX). A signal entering Port 1 exits Port 2 with ~0.5 dB loss. A reflection entering Port 2 exits Port 3. Port 1→3 is isolated by ~20 dB — the TX cannot blast the sensitive receiver directly.

Why it matters

  • The TX outputs 1 Watt. The backscatter signal is ~−60 dBm. That's a 90 dB difference. The circulator provides the first 20 dB of isolation.
  • Without it, the receiver would be completely overwhelmed by TX leakage.
  • Additional TX-RX isolation comes from the IQ mixer's balance and digital cancellation.

Signal at this point

Port 1 in: +30 dBm CW from PA.

Port 2 out: +29.5 dBm CW to antenna (0.5 dB insertion loss).

Port 2 in: Backscatter from tag, ~−60 dBm.

Port 3 out: Backscatter to receiver, ~−60.5 dBm, plus TX leakage at ~+10 dBm.
