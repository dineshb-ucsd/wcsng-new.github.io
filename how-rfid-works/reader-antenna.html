What it is physically

Typically a circularly-polarized patch antenna — a flat panel about 25 cm × 25 cm. It's a copper patch on a ground plane separated by a dielectric. A coaxial or microstrip feedline carries the signal from the circulator to the patch. Circular polarization means it works regardless of tag orientation.

What it does

The patch acts as a resonant cavity at 915 MHz. Currents on the patch create fringing fields at the edges, which radiate. The antenna has ~6 dBi gain — it focuses power into a forward-directed beam rather than radiating equally in all directions. With 1W input, the EIRP is about 4W.

Why it matters

  • Antenna gain directly extends read range — every 3 dB of gain adds ~40% more distance.
  • Circular polarization avoids orientation-dependent nulls, at the cost of 3 dB compared to a perfectly aligned linear tag.
  • The antenna pattern determines the read zone — too narrow misses tags, too wide wastes power.

Signal at this point

Input: ~1W at 50Ω coaxial, guided wave.

Output: Electromagnetic wave in free space, EIRP ≈ 4W (+36 dBm). The signal is no longer in a wire — it's a propagating E and H field.
